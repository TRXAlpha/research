# Claims, ethics, and safety

## Terminology

Preferred terms:

- affective state;
- emotion-like functional state;
- endogenous affect dynamics;
- synthetic homeostasis;
- neural metacontrol;
- biologically inspired or biologically constrained.

Avoid without extraordinary evidence:

- the model feels;
- genuine emotion;
- artificial amygdala/dopamine as literal homology;
- consciousness or sentience;
- human-like mind;
- proof of an inner life.

## Scientific risks

- Anthropomorphic interpretation of self-reports.
- Circular measurement when intervention and probe share a direction.
- Linguistic leakage from emotion-labeled prompts.
- Benchmark contamination.
- Evaluator bias and LLM-as-judge circularity.
- Mistaking an adaptive recurrent controller for an emotion-specific mechanism.
- Cherry-picking beneficial states while hiding impairments.
- Treating biological metaphors as mechanistic equivalence.

## Engineering risks

- Runaway feedback and activation collapse.
- Persistent negative-state lock-in.
- Catastrophic online updates.
- Increased sycophancy or manipulative emotional expression.
- Unstable behavior after distribution shift.
- Cross-session state leakage.

## Required safeguards

- Bound every state and modulation gain.
- Keep a hard reset and episode boundary.
- Log every hidden-state transition and intervention.
- Begin with a frozen backbone and no online weight updates.
- Separate internal affect from user-targeted emotional persuasion.
- Do not deploy the experimental system for mental-health support.
- Use synthetic environments before human interaction.
- Report failure cases and state-lock incidents.

## Human studies

Any later study involving participants, emotional manipulation, personal data, or claims about wellbeing requires institutional ethics review, informed consent, data minimization, and a withdrawal procedure.
